21.01- CCTV System Installation, Breakdown Rectification, Maintenance & Monitoring Operations

1. Purpose

This SOP defines the standard procedures for the installation, preventive maintenance, breakdown/fault rectification, and continuous monitoring of the company’s CCTV surveillance system. It ensures consistent service quality and maximum system uptime.

2. Scope

This SOP applies to all CCTV cameras, recorders (NVR/DVR), network video infrastructure, monitoring workstations, and associated cabling and power systems within the company premises and outside. It applies to all personnel involved in CCTV operations: the Security & Network Engineer, the two CCTV Technicians, and the CCTV Monitoring Operator.

3. Responsibility

Role

Responsibility

Network and Security System Engineer
  • Owns the CCTV system end-to-end and is responsible for all departmental operations.
  • Plans installations and upgrades.
  • Assigns and supervises technicians.
  • Reviews maintenance and fault reports.
  • Liaises with vendors and management.
  • Ensure all operations and activities are conducted in full compliance with data protection requirements.
Technicians
  • Carries out installation work (mounting, cabling, termination, configuration).
  • Performs scheduled preventive maintenance.
  • Attends breakdown calls and carries out fault diagnosis and rectification.
  • Replaces faulty hardware.
  • Reports unresolved issues to the Engineer.
Admin Assistant: CCTV System Operation
  • Continuously monitors live camera feeds.
  • Conducts daily CCTV system health checks and confirms all cameras and equipment are fully operational
  • Verifies the recording status of all channels
  • Immediately reports camera offline, video loss, or faulty alerts to technicians.
  • Maintain the monitoring log and incident register.
  • Update maintenance and fault logs.
  • Prepares and submits monthly reports as required by the Managing Director.
  • Reviews approved footage requests and prepare a report.

Preventive Maintenance
  • Clean all camera lenses and NVRs and check bracket/mount stability. (Weekly / Monthly)
  • cable condition, firmware updates, Dust and clean the server rack, access/login review, backup power test, check NVR and switch health status. (Quarterly)
  • Switch off and disconnect the electrical supply to all maintenance equipment before carrying out any maintenance work.
  • Reconnect the power supply and verify that all equipment is functioning correctly.

7. Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Always switch off the power at the source before handling cabling or electrical connections.
  • Use proper ladders/lifting equipment and PPE (helmet, gloves, safety harness) when working at height.
  • Apply a two-person rule for installations above 3 meters or near live electrical panels.
  • Follow site electrical safety rules and building access procedures at all times.
  • Default manufacturer passwords on all IP cameras, NVRs, and switches must be changed to complex strings immediately during commissioning.
  • The CCTV system must run on a dedicated, isolated Virtual Local Area Network (VLAN) completely separate from the primary corporate data network or guest Wi-Fi.
  • All NVRs, DVRs, and network storage pools must be housed in a locked, climate-controlled server room or secure enclosure to prevent physical tampering.
  • Install a dedicated camera facing the server rack to record all physical access and door openings
  • CCTV system credentials must not be shared and must be changed periodically
  • All authorized users must use individual login credentials. Account and password sharing are strictly prohibited.
  • Connect all main CCTV equipment (NVR and switches) to a UPS (Battery Backup). This keeps the cameras working during a power cut and protects them from electricity spikes.
  • The CCTV main monitoring room must remain fully secured at all times. Unauthorized entry is strictly prohibited
  • Report any unsafe site condition to the Security & Network Engineer before proceeding with work.

8. Non-Conformance Handling

Examples of non-conformance:

  1. Hardware/Performance: Camera and NVR (Network Video Recorder) performance faults, including intermittent recording gaps, video lagging, lens blurriness, foggy footage, total image loss, or failure of mechanical PTZ (Pan-Tilt-Zoom) components.
  2. Installation Defects: Defective installation due to unbranded or low-quality equipment and cabling, exposed wiring runs, loose structural mounting brackets, and improperly crimped RJ45 network connectors.
  3. Insufficient storage / Storage issue
  4. Power issue
  5. Software and System Issues
  6. Compliance and Data Protection Issues

Corrective Actions:

  1. Repair or replace faulty parts: Diagnose the root cause; clean, repair, or replace faulty components; or submit a warranty claim to the vendor for confirmed manufacturing defects.
  2. Re-cable using branded lines inside protective conduits, tighten all structural brackets, and re-crimp or replace loose connectors.
  3. Upgrade HDDs and replace the defective hard drive
  4. Restore power supply and check, test, and replace UPS batteries
  5. Update firmware and software to the latest version
  6. Restrict and review all access permissions

21.02-Network & IT Support Operations

10. Purpose

This SOP defines the standard procedures for the networking infrastructure and IT support functions are planned, executed, and maintained in the organisation's technology environment, ensuring uninterrupted service delivery. It ensures consistent, efficient, and accountable delivery of all networking and IT support services within the organisation

11. Scope

This SOP applies to all network and IT support activities carried out within the company premises and outside. It applies to all personnel involved in the Network & Security System Engineer, Network Technicians, and IT Support Personnel

12. Responsibility

Role

Responsibility

Network & Security System Engineer
  • Overall responsibility for network Design and planning, network architecture, configuration, and infrastructure upgrades.
  • Coordinate with ISP (SLT team) for circuit provisioning, configuration, and fault resolution.
  • Equipment procurement approval.
  • Review maintenance and critical network breakdowns.
Network Technician
  • Carries out physical network installation (cabling, patching, rack mounting and equipment installation).
  • Performs scheduled preventive maintenance.
  • Attends breakdown calls and carries out fault diagnosis and rectification.
  • Reports unresolved issues to the Engineer.
IT Support Person
  • Provide IT support to end users for hardware, software, and connectivity issues.
  • Repair and maintain computers, laptops, and printers.
  • Diagnose and rectify software issues, including OS, applications, and drivers.
  • Raise purchase requests for replacement parts or new equipment.
  • Reports unresolved issues to the Network & Security System Engineer.
  • Maintains an IT asset log.

16. Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Always switch off the power at the source before handling cabling or electrical connections.
  • Use proper ladders/lifting equipment and PPE (helmet, gloves, safety harness) when working at height.
  • Apply a two-person rule for installations above 3 meters or near live electrical panels.
  • No configuration changes to any network device without approval from the engineer.
  • No equipment purchases without written management approval. Verbal approvals are not accepted.
  • All ISP and vendor on-site visits must be supervised by a company technician.
  • All user data must be backed up before any OS reinstallation or major hardware work.
  • Network and IT system credentials must not be shared and must be changed periodically.
  • Report any unsafe site condition to the Engineer before proceeding with any work.
  • Follow all site electrical safety rules and building access procedures at all times.

17. Non-Conformance Handling

Examples of non-conformance:

  • Network slowness and application delays caused by bandwidth congestion and infrastructure performance issue.
  • Unauthorized configuration changes made to network devices or servers.
  • Unwanted software installed on company devices without proper authorization from the IT department.
  • Equipment purchases without obtaining written approval from management and the engineer.
  • Unauthorized personnel access to the network room without proper authorization or supervision.

Corrective Actions:

  • Installs Backup internet connection and activates and optimizes device configurations, blocks unwanted web applications, and continuously monitors LAN, Wi-Fi, and ISP bandwidth.
  • Configuration settings restore, access permissions reviews and updates, installs firewall and monitoring.
  • Devices scanning and cleaning, endpoint security controls updating, and restricting user account permissions
  • Procurement is corrected to require management and engineer approval.
  • Installs access control, and access is restricted to authorized personnel only

21.03-GPS System Installation/Monitoring Operations and Vehicle Audio Setup Installation.

19. Purpose

This SOP defines the standard processes for monitoring the GPS vehicle tracking system, coordinating with vendors, executing device installations, and installing vehicle audio systems.

20. Scope

This SOP applies to all company vehicles fitted with GPS tracking devices and sensors, all GPS monitoring activities, Breakdown Rectification, vendor coordination for GPS, and installation of vehicle audio systems within company premises and outside. It applies to all personnel involved in the Security & Network Engineer, the two Technicians, and the GPS Monitoring Operator.

21. Responsibility

Role

Responsibility

Network and Security System Engineer
  • Owns the GPS and Audio System end-to-end and is responsible for all departmental operations.
  • Coordinates with the GPS vendor regarding maintenance agreements, quotations, installations, and fault resolution.
  • Assigns and supervises technicians.
  • Review maintenance and fault reports.
Technicians
  • Vehicle audio system installation, wiring, testing, and handover.
Admin Assistant: GPS System Operation
  • Real-time tracking /alert monitoring.
  • Conduct daily GPS health checks and confirm all GPS devices and sensors are fully operational
  • Immediately report device breakdown, system issues, and faulty alerts to the vendor.
  • Raise purchase requests for replacement parts or new equipment.
  • Maintain the monitoring log and incident register.
  • Update maintenance and fault logs.
  • Prepare and submit monthly reports as required by the Managing Director.
  • Immediately reports an unusual incident to the engineer

24. Procedure

Steps

Description

GPS System Monitoring
  • GPS System Monitoring – live vehicle location, speed, ignition status, sensor readings, and route
  • Conduct daily GPS health checks and confirm all GPS devices and sensors are fully operational.
  • Verify accuracy of all alerts, reports and data in the GPS application.
  • Immediately reports Device/sensor offline and application issues to the vendor.
  • Maintains the monitoring log, breakdown log and incident log.
  • Updates vendor maintenance and breakdown rectification logs.
  • Reports abnormal events to the HOD.
  • Prepare and submit monthly reports as required by the Managing Director.
GPS System Installation
  • Receive the installation request from the requesting HOD.
  • Obtain approval from the Managing Director for installation requests and the vendor quotations.
  • Manage and supervise vendor installation.
  • Test and verify the accuracy of real-time data transmission, device/sensors setting, and application configurations.
  • Obtain sign-off from the Engineer to close the installation job and proceed with payment processing.
Vehicle Audio Installation and Breakdown
  • Receive the installation request from the requesting HOD.
  • Prepare a bill of materials, including Android/Car MP3 players, speakers, reverse cameras, power supply kits, and custom mounting brackets
  • Obtain approval from the Managing Director for installation requests and proceed with purchasing.
  • Perform installation, wiring, and testing of vehicle audio systems per technical specifications.
  • Conduct final quality checks with the driver to verify vehicle audio system functionality, sound clarity, and user settings optimization.
  • Document the audio system installation on the job card and obtain the driver's sign-off(vehicle number, installed items).
  • Attends breakdowns and carries out fault diagnosis and rectification.
  • Replaces faulty hardware.

25. Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Technicians must follow standard vehicle electrical safety protocols during installation.
  • All wiring must avoid interference with existing vehicle systems.

26. Non-Conformance Handling

Examples of non-conformance:

  1. Hardware/Performance faults: Performance issues affecting the audio system, speakers, reverse camera, GPS devices, and associated sensors.
  2. Installation Defects: Defective installation due to unbranded or low-quality equipment and cabling, poor cable termination, and loose or improperly fixed mounting brackets.

Corrective Actions:

  1. Repair or replace faulty parts: Diagnose the fault, restore or replace defective components, and coordinate with the vendor for warranty replacement of manufacturing defects.
  2. Replace or reinstall faulty equipment and cabling, tighten all mounting brackets, and re-terminate loose or improperly connected cables
