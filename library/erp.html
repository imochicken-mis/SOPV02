SOP-ERP-01.01 : ERP Governance and Administration

  1. Purpose

To establish a formal governance framework for managing the company's IT infrastructure+ERP system, business applications, digital information, digital transformation, system users, technology investments, and IT-related risks.

This SOP ensures that ERP activities support the company's operational and strategic objectives while maintaining system security, reliability, data integrity, accountability, regulatory compliance, and business continuity.

  1. Scope

This SOP applies to:

  • ERP systems related all IT infrastructures, and business applications.
  • All departments using the ERP system, including:
    • Breeder Farm
    • Hatchery
    • Feed Mill
    • Commercial & Buy-Back Farms
    • Live Bird Receiving
    • Processing Plant
    • Quality Assurance
    • Finished Goods & Cold Storage
    • Sales & Distribution
    • Finance
    • Human Resources
    • Procurement
    • Stores
    • Transport
    • Maintenance
  • ERP databases, servers, cloud services, network infrastructure, mobile devices, reporting tools, integrations, cybersecurity systems, and backup solutions.
  1. Responsibility

Role

Responsibility

CEO / General Manager

Approve IT strategy, policies, major investments, and critical ERP changes

Head of ERP

Overall governance of ERP applications & related IT infrastructures, Data security, and performance by system uptime availability.

Define business requirements and validate ERP controls

ERP Manager / Administrator / System Specialist / ERP Super Users

ERP configuration, workflow management, master data administration, monthly QA analysis and application support, First-level functional support, user training, and User Acceptance Testing (UAT)

Manage responsibilities for Digitally Transformed Business Process

IT Officer

Manage infrastructure, networks, servers, backups, security, and technical support

Department Heads

Approve user Transactions, departmental requirements, and process improvements

Finance Department

Verify ERP configurations affecting financial controls and statutory compliance

Internal Audit

Review system controls, access management, inventory verification and SOP compliance

All ERP Users

Use systems only for authorized business purposes and comply with ERP daily routines & policies

  1. Definitions

Term

Meaning

ERP

Enterprise Resource Planning system integrating operational and financial business processes

ERP Governance

Framework for managing technology decisions, oversee IT security risks, and performance.

Process Owner

Master Data

Core reference data such as Items, Customers, Suppliers, Farms, GL Accounts, etc.

Production Environment

Live ERP system used for business transactions

User Acceptance Testing (UAT)

Business validation performed before releasing system changes

  1. Procedure

Steps

Description

ERP Requirement Identification

1. Department Head identifies ERP system requirements.
2. System requirement is documented and submitted to ERP Department using the approved request form.
3. Request is classified as Operational Support, Enhancement, Infrastructure, Security, or Strategic Initiative.

Requirement Assessment

1. IT & ERP Department evaluates technical feasibility.
2. ERP Administrator and Process Owner assess business impact.
3. Finance reviews financial control implications where applicable.
4. Security risks and compliance requirements are assessed.
5. Estimated resources, budget, and implementation timeline are prepared.

Approval Process

1. Submit assessment to authorized management according to approval matrix.
2. Obtain necessary approvals before development or procurement begins.
3. Assign responsible ERP personnel for implementation.

System Development & Configuration

1. Configure ERP or develop solution according to approved requirements.
2. Follow relevant SOPs for development, configuration, procurement, or integration.
3. Ensure all configurations are documented.

Testing & User Acceptance

1. Conduct technical testing.
2. Perform User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with Process Owners.
3. Record testing evidence and obtain formal sign-off before production deployment.

Production Deployment

1. Deploy approved solution into Production Environment.
2. Update technical documentation, user manuals, and training materials.
3. Notify affected users of system changes.

Monitoring & Review

1. Monitor system performance and incidents.
2. Review ERP controls periodically.
3. Report governance performance to Management.
4. Maintain governance records and review continuous improvement opportunities.

  1. Safety & Compliance Requirements
  • All ERP changes shall follow approved Change Management procedures.
  • Shared user accounts are prohibited unless formally approved for system services.
  • Role-Based Access Control shall be implemented.
  • Segregation of Duties shall be maintained across all ERP system processes.
  • Master Data changes require authorized approval.
  • Transaction deletion is prohibited unless specifically authorized.
  • Critical ERP transactions shall maintain complete audit trails.
  • Financial system changes require Finance Department approval.
  • All critical ERP changes shall undergo testing before production deployment.
  • Production database modifications are prohibited except under approved emergency procedures.
  • All critical systems shall be protected through regular backup and cybersecurity controls.
  1. Non-Conformance Handling

Non-Conformance

Action

Unauthorized ERP configuration changes

Immediately reverse changes, investigate, and obtain management approval

Unauthorized Production access

Suspend user access and conduct security investigation

Unapproved Master Data changes

Restore previous data and perform root cause analysis

Failed User Acceptance Testing

Return change request for correction before deployment

Missing approval documentation

Reject implementation until approvals are completed

Critical system failure

Activate Incident Management and Business Continuity procedures

Repeated policy violations

Escalate to Management and Human Resources for disciplinary action

SOP-ERP-02.01 : User Account Creation, Modification & Deactivation

  1. Purpose

To establish a controlled process for creating, modifying, suspending, reviewing, and deactivating user accounts across the company's ERP systems.

This SOP ensures that:

  • System access is granted only to authorized users.
  • User access matches job responsibilities.
  • Segregation of Duties conflicts are prevented.
  • User accounts are promptly updated following transfers or promotions.
  • Resigned, terminated, or inactive employees cannot access company systems.
  • Temporary and third-party access is properly controlled.
  • All user account activities are traceable, auditable, and compliant with company security policies.
  1. Scope

This SOP applies to:

  • Permanent employees
  • Contract employees
  • Temporary staff
  • Consultants
  • ERP Vendors
  • Third-party service providers

It covers user accounts for:

  • ERP System
  • Finance
  • HR & Payroll
  • Sales
  • Procurement
  • Stores
  • Farms
  • Hatchery
  • Feed Mill
  • Processing
  • Finished Goods
  • QA
  • Maintenance
  • Transport
  • Reporting
  • Email
  • Windows Domain
  • VPN
  • Cloud Applications
  • Database Accounts
  • Other Company Information Systems
  1. Responsibility

Role

Notify new hires, transfers, resignations, and employment status changes

Department Head (HOD)

Internal Audit

Third-Party Sponsor

  1. Procedure

Steps

Description

New User Account Creation

1. HR confirms new employee appointment.

2. HOD submits User Access Request Form.

3. Required ERP modules and roles are specified.

4. Obtain approvals from HOD and Process Owner where applicable.

5. ERP Administrator verifies employee information.

6. Perform Segregation of Duties assessment.

7. Create unique User ID.

8. Assign approved roles.

9. Configure authentication and initial password.

10. Securely communicate login credentials.

11. User changes initial password upon first login.

12. Record account creation in the User Account Register.

User Account Modification

1. HR notifies transfer, promotion, or role change.

2. Review existing user access.

3. Remove outdated permissions.

4. HOD approves new access requirements.

5. Perform Segregation of Duties review.

6. Assign new approved roles.

7. Update User Account Register.

8. Verify access with user and HOD.

Temporary User Access

1. Submit Temporary Access Request.

2. Specify business justification and expiry date.

3. Obtain HOD and IT approval.

4. Create temporary account with limited permissions.

5. Automatically disable account on expiry date where possible.

6. Remove access after completion of assignment.

Third-Party / Vendor Access

1. Submit Third-Party Access Request.

2. Assign internal sponsor.

3. Obtain IT Management approval.

4. Create individual vendor account.

5. Restrict access to required systems only.

6. Monitor all vendor activities.

7. Disable account immediately after work completion.

User Account Deactivation

1. HR notifies resignation, termination, suspension, or contract expiry.

2. Disable user account immediately or on the effective separation date.

3. Remove VPN, email, ERP, cloud, and network access.

4. Recover company IT assets where applicable.

5. Update User Account Register and Deactivation Register.

6. Retain records according to company policy.

Periodic Access Review

1. Conduct quarterly review of ERP user accounts.

2. Compare HR Active Employee List with ERP User List.

3. Review high-risk user roles and SoD conflicts.

4. Disable dormant or unauthorized accounts.

5. Document review results and corrective actions.

Emergency Access Management

1. Submit Emergency Access Request.

2. Obtain Management approval.

3. Grant temporary elevated access.

4. Log all activities performed.

5. Remove elevated access immediately after incident resolution.

6. Conduct post-incident review.

  1. Safety & Compliance Requirements
  1. All user accounts shall have formal approval before creation.
  2. User IDs shall be unique and assigned to one individual only.
  3. Shared accounts shall be prohibited unless technically unavoidable.
  4. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) shall be implemented.
  5. Segregation of Duties shall be verified before assigning access.
  6. Temporary access shall include expiry dates.
  7. User account activities shall be logged and retained for audit purposes.
  8. All user access shall comply with the Company's Information Security Policy.
    1. Non-Conformance Handling

Non-Conformance

Disable immediately and conduct investigation

SOP-ERP-03.01 : ERP Role & Access Rights Management

To establish a controlled process for designing, approving, assigning, modifying, reviewing, and removing ERP roles and access rights to ensure users have only the minimum permissions required to perform their job responsibilities.

This SOP ensures:

  • Role-Based Access Control is implemented throughout the ERP system.
  • Segregation of Duties conflicts are prevented.
  • High-risk ERP permissions are properly controlled.
  • ERP roles are assigned according to business responsibilities.
  • ERP role changes are approved, documented, and auditable.
  • Unauthorized access to business and financial data is prevented.

    1. Procedure

Steps

Description

ERP Role Creation

1. Department Head identifies the requirement for a new ERP role.

2. ERP Administrator verifies whether an existing role can be used.

3. Process Owner defines required functions and permissions.

4. Determine View, Create, Edit, Approve, Post and Reverse permissions.

5. Define operational data scope (Company, Farm, Warehouse, Department, etc.).

6. Conduct Segregation of Duties (SoD) assessment.

7. Identify high-risk permissions.

8. Obtain approvals from HOD, Process Owner and Finance (where applicable).

11. Deploy the approved role to the production ERP system.

12. Update the ERP Role Register.

ERP Role Assignment

1. Receive approved User Access Request.

2. Assign approved ERP role to the user.

3. Verify assigned permissions.

4. Notify the user and Department Head.

5. Record role assignment in the User–Role Assignment Register.

Role Modification

1. Submit Role Change Request.

2. Assess business and system impact.

3. Perform Segregation of Duties review.

4. Obtain approvals.

5. Configure changes in Test Environment.

6. Conduct testing and UAT.

7. Deploy approved changes.

8. Update all related documentation and registers.

Role Review & Certification

1. Generate ERP User Role Report periodically.

2. Review assigned roles with Department Heads.

3. Verify user responsibilities and permissions.

4. Remove obsolete or excessive access.

5. Document review results.

Temporary Elevated Access

1. Submit Temporary Elevated Access Request.

2. Obtain Management approval.

3. Assign temporary permissions with expiry date.

4. Log all activities.

5. Remove elevated access immediately after completion.

ERP Role Cleanup

1. Review duplicate, unused and obsolete roles.

2. Identify roles without owners.

3. Remove expired temporary roles.

4. Disable obsolete roles after approval.

5. Update ERP Role Register.

  1. Safety & Compliance Requirements
  • ERP access shall follow the Principle of Least Privilege.
  • Role-Based Access Control shall be implemented.
  • Segregation of Duties shall be verified before assigning ERP roles.
  • Self-approval of transactions shall be prohibited wherever possible.
  • High-risk permissions shall require additional approval.
  • ERP Administrators shall not perform normal business approvals.
  • Direct database modifications shall be prohibited except under approved emergency procedures.
  • Temporary elevated permissions shall include an expiry date.
  • ERP role changes shall follow Change Management procedures.
  • ERP role reviews shall be conducted periodically.
  1. Non-Conformance Handling

Non-Conformance

Action

Remove role immediately and investigate

Unauthorized role modification

Restore previous configuration and conduct investigation

Segregation of Duties conflict detected

Remove conflicting permissions and reassess user access

Excessive user permissions

Reduce access to approved role level

Temporary elevated access not removed

Remove access immediately and review procedures

Unauthorized database modification

Investigate, restore data and report to Management

ERP Administrator performing unauthorized business transactions

Conduct investigation and apply disciplinary action

SOP-ITERP-04 — Password, Authentication and Privileged Access Management

1.Purpose

To establish a secure process for managing passwords, authentication methods, and privileged user accounts across the company's ERP systems, IT infrastructure, databases, cloud applications, and other digital platforms.

This SOP ensures:

  • Only authorized users can access company systems.
  • Passwords and authentication methods are securely managed.
  • Privileged accounts are properly controlled and monitored.
  • Unauthorized system access and credential misuse are prevented.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication is implemented for high-risk systems.
  • Authentication activities are traceable and auditable.

2.Scope

Description

Vendor & Remote Access

Emergency Access

1. Submit emergency access request.

2. Obtain authorized approval where feasible.

3. Use emergency account only for recovery activities.

4. Log all actions performed.

5. Disable emergency access after incident resolution.

6. Conduct management review.

Authentication Monitoring

1. Monitor login attempts, password resets, account lockouts, privileged logins, and MFA failures.
2. Investigate unusual authentication activities.
3. Escalate suspected security incidents according to Information Security procedures.

7.Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Every user shall authenticate using an approved individual account.
  • Passwords shall never be shared.
  • Default passwords shall be changed before production use.
  • MFA shall be enabled for high-risk accounts wherever supported.
  • Administrator accounts shall be separate from normal user accounts.
  • Privileged access shall follow the Principle of Least Privilege.
  • Vendor administrative access shall be temporary and monitored.
  • Password resets require identity verification.
  • Authentication logs shall be retained for audit purposes.
  • Service accounts shall have designated owners and documented purposes.
  • Emergency accounts shall not be used for routine work.

SOP-ERP-05 : IT Helpdesk & User Support Management

1.Purpose

To establish a standardized process for receiving, recording, prioritizing, assigning, resolving, escalating, and closing IT and ERP user support requests.

This SOP ensures:

  • All IT and ERP support requests are properly recorded and traceable.
  • Business-critical incidents receive timely attention.
  • Users have approved channels to request IT support.
  • Support requests are resolved within agreed service levels.
  • Recurring issues are identified and corrective actions implemented.
  • ERP support activities do not result in unauthorized data or system changes.
  • IT service performance can be monitored and continually improved.

2.Scope

This SOP applies to:

  • All ERP Users
  • IT Helpdesk Personnel
  • ERP Support Team
  • ERP Administrators
  • System Administrators
  • Network Administrators
  • Database Administrators
  • Department Heads
  • Vendors and Third-Party Support Personnel

It covers:

  • ERP Functional Support
  • User Account & Login Issues
  • Desktop & Laptop Support
  • Printer & Peripheral Support
  • Network & Internet Issues
  • Email Systems
  • Servers & Databases
  • Mobile Devices & Applications
  • System Integrations
  • Reporting & Business Intelligence
  • CCTV & Access Control Interfaces
  • Weighbridge & Industrial Interfaces
  • Other Company IT Systems

Overall management of IT Helpdesk operations, SLA monitoring, and major incident escalation

6.Procedure

Steps

Description

Correction Hierarchy

Corrections shall follow the preferred order:

(1) Correct before posting — edit/validate/approve;

(2) Cancel and recreate where ERP permits;

(3) Reverse and repost for posted transactions;

(4) Approved adjustment entry where reversal is not possible;

(5) Controlled data amendment via an authorized ERP mechanism;

(6) Direct database correction — exceptional last resort only.

Standard Correction Process

1. User/Department identifies the error and raises a correction request referencing the original transaction.

2. Requester describes the correct value, reason and supporting evidence.

3. HOD/Process Owner validates; ERP + Finance assess operational/financial impact.

4. ERP Functional Officer determines the correction method and obtains required approvals.

5. Authorized user executes the reversal/correction.

6. Business and Finance validate inventory, production and financial impact.

7. Correction is recorded in the register and the request closed after verification.

Pre-Posting Correction

Where a transaction has not yet been approved or posted (e.g. a draft Purchase Requisition with a wrong quantity), it is edited, validated and resubmitted within the normal workflow — no reversal is required, though change history should be retained where possible.

Post-Posting Correction (Reverse & Repost)

Once a transaction has affected inventory, GL, production, costing, payroll or supplier/customer balances, the original record remains historically visible and is corrected via: Original Transaction → Reversal → Correct Transaction. Applies to GRNs, GINs, sales invoices, supplier invoices and journal entries — e.g. an incorrect GRN quantity is verified against delivery documents, then reversed/adjusted rather than directly edited in the database.

Stock Adjustment

1. Physical count and reason code obtained (Damage, Expiry, Count Difference, Conversion Error, Production Variance, Approved Write-Off, System Correction, etc.).

2. Root cause investigated (GRN, transfer, GIN, sales return, damage, write-off, counting/UOM error, duplicate entry) before any adjustment.

3. Maker-checker control applied; material adjustments reviewed by Finance and approved by HOD, with full audit trail.

Backdated Transaction / Closed Period Correction

1. Backdating requests specify the reason for delay, correct effective date, affected period and financial impact, with Finance approval required for closed/reporting periods.

2. Where the period is closed, correction is preferably posted in the current open period; period reopening is exceptional and requires Finance assessment, CFO/authorized approval, controlled reopening, correction, validation and reclosing with audit log review.

Duplicate Transaction Management

1. Suspected duplicates (from repeat entry, retries, interface reprocessing, or system timeout) are verified against source document number, external reference, batch ID or interface message ID to confirm which transaction is valid.

2. The duplicate is reversed and the interface/root cause investigated and reconciled separately from the correction itself.

Direct Database Amendment

Used only when no standard ERP correction mechanism exists and impact is material. Process: formal request → functional verification → technical/vendor assessment → risk/impact assessment → Management/IT approval → database backup/snapshot → documented change method → execution by authorized DBA → recording of old/new values → post-change validation → reconciliation of related data → audit/system log review → closure with evidence retained.

Correction Hierarchy

Corrections shall follow the preferred order:

(1) Correct before posting — edit/validate/approve;

(2) Cancel and recreate where ERP permits;

(3) Reverse and repost for posted transactions;

(4) Approved adjustment entry where reversal is not possible;

(5) Controlled data amendment via an authorized ERP mechanism;

(6) Direct database correction — exceptional last resort only.

7.Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Posted transactions shall not be silently overwritten; the original transaction shall remain traceable.
  • Every correction shall require documented business justification and supporting evidence.
  • High-risk corrections shall require independent approval; financial corrections require Finance validation.
  • Inventory corrections require physical/transaction validation; poultry batch corrections require process-owner validation.
  • Closed-period changes shall be tightly controlled; period reopening is exceptional and requires CFO/authorized approval.
  • Direct database changes shall be exceptional, and always require backup and documented audit evidence.
  • Corrections shall be reconciled across all dependent modules (e.g. inventory, costing, GL, cost centre) before being considered closed.
  • Duplicate transactions shall be investigated using unique source/interface references before reversal.
  • Correction reason codes shall be recorded to support trend analysis.
  • Repeated or high-risk corrections shall be monitored by user, department, module and reason code.
  • Payment reversals shall be executed by authorized Finance personnel only, never by IT.
  • Suspicious correction patterns (repeated write-offs, backdated entries, frequent credit notes, mortality reductions, post-close journals, corrections by privileged users, or corrections lacking supporting documents) shall trigger enhanced review and, where fraud is suspected, escalation under company investigation procedures.

SOP-ERP-10 : ERP Workflow and Approval Configuration Management

1.Purpose

To establish a controlled process for the design, approval, configuration, modification, testing, activation and review of ERP workflows and approval authorities.

This SOP ensures that:

  • Approval workflows reflect approved business processes and authority limits are correctly enforced
  • Self-approval is prevented and segregation of duties is maintained
  • Temporary approvers are controlled and workflow changes are formally approved and tested
  • Approval history remains fully traceable and transactions cannot bypass approved authorization levels
  • Core Principle: ERP workflows shall enforce approved business authority and internal controls; the system shall not be configured merely for convenience.

2.Scope

This SOP applies to workflows across the poultry business, including Breeder, Hatchery, Feed Mill, Commercial/Buy-Back Farm, Processing, QA, Finished Goods, Sales, Procurement, Stores, Maintenance, Transport, HR, Finance and Administration.

It covers workflows such as:

  • Purchase Requisition, Purchase Order, Material Request, GIN, GRN
  • Stock adjustment and inventory transfer
  • Sales order, price/discount approval, customer credit approval, sales return, credit note
  • Payment approval, journal entry approval and payroll approval
  • Master-data approval and feed-formula approval
  • Poultry batch adjustment, capex approval, maintenance request and user-access approval

6.Procedure

Steps

Description

New Workflow Configuration

1. Process Owner identifies the business process and documents process steps with ERP.

2. Maker/Checker/Approver roles and approval limits/conditions defined (Management/Finance).

3. SoD conflicts reviewed by ERP/Internal Control; workflow specification prepared and business-approved.

4. Configured and tested in a test environment; UAT performed and signed off by Process Owner.

5. Production deployment approved by Authorized Authority, deployed, verified live, and recorded in the Workflow Register.

Approval Matrix & Value-Based Approval

1. A formal Approval Matrix is maintained, linked to the Delegation of Authority, defining Maker/Checker/Approver/Final Authority per transaction type.

2. Workflows support value thresholds (e.g. HOD up to a limit, HOD + Functional Manager at the next tier, Finance + Senior Management above that) per the company's approved authority matrix.

3. Conditional criteria beyond value (category, capex/opex, location, new supplier, non-budgeted purchase, QA hold/release, etc.) are applied where relevant.

Self-Approval Control & Maker-Checker

1. The ERP prohibits self-approval wherever practical (e.g. a requester cannot approve their own PR; a storekeeper cannot finalize their own stock adjustment).

2. Higher-risk transactions follow Maker → Checker → Approver → Posting so no single user controls the full transaction lifecycle (e.g. supplier payments).

3. Any self-approval exception is formally approved and monitored.

Approval by Organizational Scope

Approvers are restricted to their authorized scope (e.g. a Farm Manager approves only their farm, a Regional Sales Manager only their territory), enforced through ERP data scope.

Substitute Approvers & Delegation

1. Temporary substitution (leave, travel, vacancy) is requested with a defined date range and authorized before activation.

2. Delegation specifies primary approver, substitute, start/end date, transaction types and authority limits.

3. A substitute cannot receive authority greater than the primary approver's own limit; delegation expires automatically or is reviewed.

Escalation & Rejected/Withdrawn Transactions

1. Pending approvals may trigger reminders and escalate to the next authorized approver after a defined period, without bypassing required approval levels.

2. Rejections require a mandatory reason, return to the appropriate user, and retain visible history; resubmission triggers the correct approval sequence.

3. Withdrawals retain original submission, withdrawal user, date/time, reason and resubmission history.

Workflow Override

Overrides are extremely limited and each one documents the reason, business urgency, affected transaction, approver, ERP administrator, date/time, impact and post-review. Routine admin approval shall not substitute for normal business authorization.

Workflow Configuration Change & Version Control

1. Any change to approver, approval limit, sequence, condition, escalation or system rule follows formal change control: Change Request → Impact Assessment → Approval → Test → UAT → Production Deployment → Verification (per SOP-ERP-12).

2. Major workflow changes are versioned (e.g. PR-WF-V01 / V02) with effective dates, so the governing version of any historical transaction can be identified.

7.Safety & Compliance Requirements

  • Workflows shall reflect approved business processes; approval limits shall align with the Delegation of Authority.
  • Self-approval shall be prevented wherever practical.
  • High-risk processes shall use maker-checker controls.
  • Workflow changes require formal approval and shall be tested before deployment.
  • Temporary delegation shall have expiry dates; users shall not delegate authority beyond their own limit.
  • Workflow overrides shall be exceptional, fully documented and logged.
  • Approval history and rejection reasons shall be retained as part of the ERP audit trail.
  • High-risk workflows shall be periodically reviewed and sampled for compliance.
  • Inactive/former employees shall not remain active approvers.
  • Workflow bottlenecks, stuck transactions and repeated escalations shall be monitored.
  • Emergency workflows shall remain exceptional and shall not become routine shortcuts.
